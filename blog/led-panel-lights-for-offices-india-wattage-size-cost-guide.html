LED Panel Lights for Offices in India — A Practical Wattage, Size & Cost Guide

How to choose LED panel lights for Indian offices — wattage per sqft, size selection, cost estimation, colour temperature. Practical 2026 guide from Solid State Lights.

Choosing the right LED panel lights for an office looks simple until you stand in the middle of a 3,000 sqft floor with pillars, low ceilings, glare complaints from the last install and a lighting budget that's already been cut twice. This guide walks a project manager, interior designer or facilities head through the actual decisions that matter — how many watts you really need per square foot, which panel sizes fit standard Indian false ceilings, how to price it accurately and where the money goes when things fail two years in.

Written from a manufacturer's perspective at Solid State Lights, based on projects across Sangli, Kolhapur, Pune, Belgavi, Thane and the rest of Maharashtra.

Why LED Panels are the Default for Offices

LED panels replaced troffers, fluorescent tubes and older recessed downlights in Indian offices for four practical reasons — uniform light distribution across a work surface, low glare when properly diffused, low profile that fits standard 600×600 mm false-ceiling grids, and low power draw compared to legacy lighting. A modern LED panel light from a reputable manufacturer will typically consume 30–40% less energy than the T5/T8 fluorescent installation it replaces, at the same or better lux levels.

The catch: not every panel labelled “LED” is engineered for office duty cycles. Cheap panels use low-CRI chips that make skin tones and print material look washed out, use drivers that fail under Indian voltage fluctuations, and use frames that warp in false-ceiling humidity. The savings evaporate the first time you replace a batch.

How Many Watts Do You Need Per Square Foot?

The Bureau of Indian Standards guideline for general office illumination is 300–500 lux at desk height. Modern LED panels deliver roughly 100–110 lumens per watt in operation. Once you factor in mounting height, ceiling reflectance and normal maintenance loss, the practical wattage-per-square-foot planning number for Indian offices sits at:

  • Open-plan work areas: ~0.75–1.0 W/sqft (target 400–500 lux)
  • Cabins and meeting rooms: ~0.9–1.2 W/sqft (target 500 lux with dimming headroom)
  • Corridors, lobbies: ~0.4–0.6 W/sqft (target 200–250 lux)
  • Reception, breakout, cafe: ~0.5–0.7 W/sqft with a warm 3000K CCT for feel

Rule of thumb for a general office fit-out: reserve 0.85 W/sqft as your baseline, then add task lighting on top for specific zones like design bays or drafting tables.

Which Panel Size Should You Choose?

Panel size choice follows ceiling grid and application, not the other way round:

  • 600×600 mm (36W or 40W): the default for grid-and-tile ceilings. Every metal ceiling and standard mineral-fibre ceiling in India is designed around this module. Buy 36W for open work areas, 40W for high-density workstations.
  • 300×1200 mm (36W or 40W): better for long meeting-room tables, corridor runs and areas where you want linear light streams instead of square patches.
  • 600×1200 mm (60W): for large open areas — call centres, training halls, banquet-style pantry zones. Halves your fixture count vs 600×600 for the same lux.
  • Round 170mm / 225mm (18W or 24W): for concrete cast ceilings where 600mm cutouts aren’t possible — small cabins, WCs, storage.

If your ceiling is gypsum board (not grid-and-tile), slim recessed round panels or surface cylinder lights are usually a better call than square panels — less cutting, faster install, cleaner finish.

Colour Temperature Decisions

Colour temperature is a productivity, comfort and brand decision, not a technical one. In Indian offices, use:

  • 6500K (Cool Daylight): engineering, R&D, drafting, hospitals, laboratories, quality-control lines — wherever visual acuity and colour discrimination matter more than warmth
  • 4000K (Natural White): the safe default for general work areas, meeting rooms, corridors — balanced feel that doesn't fatigue over an 8-hour day
  • 3000K (Warm White): reception, lobbies, cafeterias, breakout zones, boutique and creative-agency workspaces where atmosphere is part of the brand

Mixing temperatures within one visible zone is almost always a mistake. If you're specifying two CCTs for one floor, they should be in physically or visually separate rooms.

Cost Estimation — What a Fit-Out Actually Costs

Panel-only material cost varies significantly by chip quality and driver quality. As a rough 2026 planning band:

  • Standard-grade 600×600, 36W: ₹700–1,200 per panel
  • Premium anti-glare / BIS-driver 600×600, 40W: ₹1,200–2,000 per panel
  • Back-lit slim panels tend to be 10–20% more than edge-lit at the same wattage

Add roughly 15–25% for wiring, driver housings, ceiling coordination and installation labour. For a 3,000 sqft general office needing ~2.55 kW of panel lighting, you're looking at 70–90 units of 36W panels — a material budget in the ₹70,000–₹1,50,000 range depending on brand tier.

Where Cheap Panels Fail (and What to Check Before You Buy)

The four failure modes we see on panels replaced within 18–24 months of install:

  1. Driver failure due to voltage spikes. Indian office feeders swing significantly — without surge-protected drivers, cheap panels are lottery tickets. Always insist on BIS-approved drivers.
  2. LGP yellowing (edge-lit panels). Poor-quality light-guide plates yellow over 12–18 months, dropping lumen output and shifting colour warmer over time.
  3. Flicker at reduced voltage. If your building has voltage drops during peak hours, low-quality drivers start flickering below 200V — noticeable and fatigue-inducing.
  4. Frame warping / mounting failure. Aluminium frames need proper heat management; thin frames with poor heat-sink design warp in humid climates.

Before you specify a brand, ask for the driver make, driver protection ratings (surge, over-voltage, short-circuit), warranty terms (in writing, not just a sticker) and BIS approval number.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many watts of LED panel lighting do I need for a 1,000 sqft office?

For a general open-plan office at ~0.85 W/sqft, plan for approximately 850W of panel lighting — typically 22–24 units of 36W 600×600 panels, targeting 400–500 lux at desk height. Add task lighting for specialised zones.

Are 600×600 or 600×1200 panels better for offices?

600×600 mm is the default for grid-and-tile ceilings because it matches the standard 60 cm ceiling module. 600×1200 mm reduces fixture count for large open areas and is a better choice for training halls and call centres above 3,000 sqft.

What CCT is best for office LED panels?

4000K (Natural White) is the safest default for general work areas. Use 6500K (Cool Daylight) for engineering / R&D / QC lines, and 3000K (Warm White) for reception, cafeteria and lobby areas.

Should I use edge-lit or back-lit panels?

Back-lit panels give more uniform light with fewer hot-spots and tend to hold their colour temperature better over 3–5 years. Edge-lit panels are slimmer and lower-cost. For long-life office installs, back-lit is worth the 10–20% premium.
